## Turning interviews into knowledge

This week, we revisited the notes and insights gathered from the experts during the September 25th session. We used their explanations of automotive credit assessment to identify the facts that our system needs in order to reason about an application. These facts are the first building blocks of our knowledge base and will later be used by the Drools rule engine.

## Building the initial fact base

We started translating the experts' knowledge into structured information about the applicant, the vehicle, and the loan conditions. This work helped us distinguish between the information provided as input and the conclusions that should be produced by the rules. It also gave us a more concrete basis for deciding which variables are relevant and how they should be represented in the system.

## Mapping the main rules

Alongside the fact base, we created a flowchart representing the main decision path of the system. The flowchart organizes the most important rules and decision boundaries, showing how the system should move from the initial application facts to outcomes such as approval, rejection, or further review. This visual model will guide the implementation of the rules in Drools and help us identify gaps or overlaps before coding them.

## Preliminary report

We also began preparing the preliminary report, which must be delivered and presented in week 4. The report brings together the problem definition, the expert knowledge elicitation process, the initial knowledge representation, and the proposed reasoning flow. Preparing it alongside the implementation work is helping us keep the system's design decisions documented and aligned with the project objectives.
