## Context & kickoff

Following the September 9 session with Professor Goreti Marreiros and Professor Carlos Ramos, we dedicated the first classes of the week to aligning on the scope and objectives for both PPROGIA and ENGCIA. Beyond setting academic expectations, this kickoff allowed us to form the team, define our team identity, and set up the foundation of our engineering blog while brainstorming directions for our first challenge.

## This week's core question

What financial decision is narrow enough to model with explicit rules, useful enough to matter, and transparent enough to evaluate?

We started with four initial candidates: **credit risk triage**, **fraud alert prioritisation**, **investment suitability**, and **loan eligibility**. Each presents a strong use case for explainable systems, but each comes with distinct constraints regarding data availability, regulatory friction, and domain complexity.

## Work completed

- Aligned on the project goals and expectations for both courses.
- Formed the team and defined our collaborative identity.
- Set up the engineering blog and established our working documentation structure.
- Compared the four initial candidate domains against a shared decision lens.
- Started drafting criteria for evaluating problem fit: scope, rule availability, explainability, data constraints, and ethical risk.

## Decision log

By the end of the week, it was established that the project will focus on **credit risk assessment**. The remaining decision is whether to address the full spectrum of credit types (personal, mortgage, and auto loans) or narrow our scope down to a single segment. While all options remain viable, they differ significantly in data availability, regulatory constraints, and ruleability within our inference engine. We will continue refining this choice by evaluating how easily each loan type can be modeled under real-world scenarios.
> An expert system is only useful when its reasoning can be inspected, challenged, and improved.

## Next week

Next, we will translate the candidate credit types into concrete evaluation scenarios, formalize the key decision boundaries, and draft the initial rule base for the most viable approach.
