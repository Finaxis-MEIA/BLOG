## This week's question

What financial decision is narrow enough to model with explicit rules, useful enough to matter, and transparent enough to evaluate?

We began with four candidates: **credit risk triage**, **fraud alert prioritisation**, **investment suitability**, and **loan eligibility**. Each has a meaningful role for explainable reasoning, but each brings different constraints around data, regulation, and domain expertise.

## Work completed

- Defined six comparison criteria: scope, rule availability, explainability, testability, data needs, and ethical risk.
- Reviewed introductory literature and public guidance for each candidate.
- Built a first decision matrix instead of choosing from intuition.
- Created a small Drools proof of concept to confirm our toolchain.

## Decision log

No final domain decision yet. Credit risk triage and investment suitability currently score highest. We will challenge both options with concrete user stories before committing.

> An expert system is only useful when its reasoning can be inspected, challenged, and improved.

## Next week

Interview potential domain experts, write representative cases, and select one problem with a clearly bounded decision output.
